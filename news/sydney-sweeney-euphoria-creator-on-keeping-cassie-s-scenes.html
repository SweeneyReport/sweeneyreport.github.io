Sydney Sweeney: What Euphoria's Creator Says About Her Scenes

Sam Levinson told The New York Times he weighed a nudity-free final season for Cassie, but Sydney Sweeney disagreed, citing the character's story.

By Sweeney Report · October 25, 2026

Euphoria creator Sam Levinson said Sydney Sweeney pushed back when he considered removing nudity from Cassie's storyline in the show's final season. Levinson made the comments to The New York Times, according to coverage from NME and other outlets. He said he had wondered whether the season could be shot without any nudity for the character, and that Sweeney did not agree with the idea.

What Levinson said

According to the reports, Levinson explained that he first thought about filming the material without nudity. Sweeney reportedly answered that Cassie had become an OnlyFans creator, so the story should not be written around that fact. Levinson said he accepted the argument and called it a fair point.

He also reportedly described Sweeney as a fearless and professional actor who arrives on set ready to work. Coverage noted that the discussion centered on a montage sequence in the season's fifth episode.

Script, auditions and SAG rules

Levinson also addressed how the requirements were communicated. He said the nudity requirements were written into the script and were raised at the audition stage. He added that actors have the right to decline such material under SAG rules. Taken together, his remarks framed the scenes as a choice made with full knowledge of the project, rather than something imposed late in production.

These are Levinson's own account of events. Sweeney had not been quoted in the coverage reviewed here, and the original interview was not independently checked by this site.

The wider conversation

Cassie's arc has been one of the most discussed parts of the series, and her move into online adult content became a major storyline in the final season. Some outlets reported that viewers and commentators were divided on how the show handled that material, with some calling certain scenes unnecessary. Others, as Levinson's comments suggest, see the content as central to the character's path.

The remarks add to a long-running public debate about how television portrays sexuality, and about how much say actors have over those portrayals. Opinions vary widely, and the interview itself does not settle that question.

What's next

With Euphoria's run now concluded, attention is likely to shift to Sweeney's upcoming projects and to whether more cast members and the creative team discuss the final season in interviews. Sweeney has not been reported to have responded directly to Levinson's latest remarks. We will update this story if she or HBO comments.
