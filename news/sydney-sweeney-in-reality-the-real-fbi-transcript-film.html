Sydney Sweeney's Reality: Every Line Is Real FBI Dialogue

HBO's 2023 film Reality has Sydney Sweeney speaking the FBI's actual interrogation transcript of Reality Winner, word for word.

By Sweeney Report · November 3, 2026

Sydney Sweeney carried the 2023 HBO film Reality with dialogue taken directly from the FBI's recorded questioning of Reality Winner, according to the film's production background as documented on Wikipedia. Sweeney played Winner, a former NSA translator working for a contractor, who was questioned by agents at her home on the day of her arrest in June 2017. Director Tina Satter built the screenplay around that transcript, which makes it one of the most unusual and serious roles of Sweeney's career.

A real transcript, word for word

The film covers roughly an hour of questioning. Wikipedia's summary of the film notes that Winner admits during the exchange to sending an NSA document to The Intercept. The report concerned Russian interference in the 2016 US election. Satter and co-writer James Paul Dallas kept the agents' and Winner's words as they appear in the record, including the awkward pauses and small talk.

The redacted portions of the transcript also appear on screen. According to the same summary, the film at first conceals those details, and later Winner's words break through the redactions.

From the stage to HBO

Satter first staged the same transcript as the 2019 play Is This a Room. Reality was her feature directing debut. Josh Hamilton and Marchánt Davis played the two FBI agents. The film premiered at the Berlin International Film Festival on February 18, 2023. HBO Films acquired US rights, and it aired on HBO on May 29, 2023. The running time is 82 minutes.

The real case

The FBI arrested Winner on June 3, 2017. According to Wikipedia, she pleaded guilty in June 2018 to one felony count of transmitting national defense information. In August 2018 she received a sentence of five years and three months. She was released from federal custody in November 2021.

Reception

Wikipedia reports that critics praised Satter's direction and Sweeney's performance. Rotten Tomatoes listed a 93% positive score from 92 reviews, and Metacritic gave the film 83 out of 100. The film also received a Peabody Award and several nominations, including from the Critics' Choice Awards, the Producers Guild of America and the Gotham Awards.

What's next: Reality remains a key reference point for Sweeney's dramatic range. Viewers can judge for themselves whether it is her most serious role.
