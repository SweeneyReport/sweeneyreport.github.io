Sydney Sweeney Chased This Horror Movie for 10 Years

Sydney Sweeney first auditioned for Immaculate in 2014, then revived it after Euphoria and produced it; the film earned about $35 million worldwide.

By Sweeney Report · November 1, 2026

Sydney Sweeney spent about a decade trying to get Immaculate made. She first auditioned for the project in 2014, when it stalled in development. After her success on Euphoria, she revived it, starred in it and produced it through her company, Fifty-Fifty Films. The film premiered at South by Southwest on March 12, 2024. Neon released it in US theaters on March 22, 2024. It had a budget of about $9 million and grossed about $35 million worldwide.

A project that stalled

According to Wikipedia, Sweeney first auditioned for a role when Andrew Lobel's screenplay was already in development. The project then sat in development limbo for years. Mohan has said the original script centered on high school girls, not nuns. /Film reported that Sweeney first read the script when she was about 15 or 16.

How she brought it back

/Film reported that after the second season of Euphoria, Sweeney wanted to start producing and knew fans were hoping for a horror film. She remembered the script and contacted Lobel, who sold it to her. She then sent it to director Michael Mohan, her frequent collaborator. Mohan told the outlet, "Syd hired me to direct this movie."

Wikipedia lists the producers as David Bernad, Sweeney, Jonathan Davino, Teddy Schwarzman and Michael Heimler. The production companies were Black Bear Pictures, Fifty-Fifty Films and Middle Child Pictures. Sweeney joined the cast in October 2022. Filming took place in and around Rome and wrapped by February 2023.

Release and box office

Neon acquired US distribution rights in December 2023. After the SXSW premiere, the film opened in US theaters on March 22, 2024. Wikipedia reports that it earned about $15.7 million in the US and Canada and about $35 million worldwide, against a $9 million budget.

Reviews were mixed to positive. Rotten Tomatoes showed 72% positive reviews from 211 critics, and Metacritic gave it a score of 57 out of 100. Audiences polled by CinemaScore gave it a "C."

What's next: Immaculate showed that Sweeney could develop and produce a film at a modest budget and make a profit at the box office. No new horror project from her was confirmed in the sources reviewed for this article.
