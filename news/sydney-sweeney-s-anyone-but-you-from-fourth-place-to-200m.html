Sydney Sweeney's Anyone But You: How a Fourth-Place Film Won

Sydney Sweeney's rom-com Anyone But You opened in fourth place with about $6 million and went on to pass $200 million worldwide on a $25 million budget.

By Sweeney Report · October 29, 2026

Anyone But You, the romantic comedy starring Sydney Sweeney and Glen Powell, started as a modest performer and became one of the most talked-about sleeper hits in recent years. The film opened in fourth place at the end of December 2023 with roughly $6 million, then went on to pass $200 million worldwide. It was made for a reported $25 million.

A slow start, then a steady climb

The opening weekend was small, at about $6.3 million domestically. Rather than fading, the film grew over the holiday period. Its second weekend reached about $8.8 million, an unusual rise for a new release. Over its run it reportedly earned more than fourteen times its opening weekend. Yahoo's coverage of Deadline's report put the domestic multiple at 14.5, which it described as Sony's best ever.

Trade coverage pointed to strong audience holds and repeat viewings, especially among younger moviegoers, and to social media as a driver of interest. Steven O'Dell, Sony Pictures Releasing International's president, said the results confirmed that the rom-com genre was "alive and kicking."

Milestones and a second life

Sony brought the film back in theaters on February 9, 2024, as Anyone But You: The Valentine Encore, a version with added bonus content. In late February, /Film reported that the film had crossed $200 million worldwide, with about $87 million from the domestic market and $113 million internationally.

According to /Film, that made it the first rom-com of any kind to pass $200 million since Crazy Rich Asians in 2018, which earned $239 million. It was also the first R-rated comedy to reach that mark since Bridget Jones's Baby in 2016.

The film then arrived on Netflix in the United States on April 23, 2024. Slashfilm and other outlets reported that it climbed to number one on the platform's chart shortly afterward, extending its reach well beyond theaters.

Why it matters

The result was a notable win for Sweeney and Powell, who also served as producers on the project, and for a genre that studios had often treated as a streaming-only bet. Sweeney has since said in interviews, as reported by People, that she and Powell have discussed ideas for a sequel, though nothing has been officially announced.

What's next: No sequel has been confirmed. For now, the film remains a reference point for how word of mouth, a holiday release window and a second wave of streaming interest can turn a quiet opening into a major box office result.
