Sydney Sweeney Joke Puts Ali G in Hot Water

Sacha Baron Cohen's in-character remark about Sydney Sweeney at the Ali G: Who Iz I? premiere reportedly drew calls for him to be cancelled.

By Sweeney Report · October 25, 2026

Sacha Baron Cohen is facing online backlash after a joke about Sydney Sweeney, made while he was in character as Ali G. IMDb reported that the remark came at the premiere of Ali G: Who Iz I?, ahead of the character's return to the big screen. According to the report, Baron Cohen was asked about a studio merger and answered with a crude line that referenced Sweeney. No comment from Sweeney has been reported.

What happened at the premiere

Per IMDb's account, the exchange took place while Baron Cohen was speaking as Ali G rather than as himself. A question about a studio merger reportedly prompted the answer that mentioned the actress. The outlet described the line as crude. This article does not repeat it, because the reported wording has not been independently confirmed here.

Ali G is known for blurring the line between satire and provocation, and the character has long relied on awkward, boundary-pushing answers. Whether the joke targeted Sweeney or simply used her name for shock value is a matter of interpretation. IMDb did not present it as a statement from her or her team.

The reaction online

IMDb reported that some people online called for Baron Cohen to be cancelled after the remark circulated. The scale of that reaction has not been quantified in the report, so it is unclear how widespread the criticism is. Other viewers may well see the line as part of the character's long-running act.

The film was reportedly already facing criticism before the premiere. Its trailer drew complaints from critics who described the Ali G character as a caricature. The Sweeney joke has now added a second source of controversy to the release.

A wider debate

The episode has revived a familiar question about whether comedians should still perform characters like Ali G, and whether in-character humor deserves different treatment from a comedian's own words. Supporters of the format typically point to its satirical purpose. Critics argue that it can come across as dated or hurtful. Both views are those of the people who hold them, and neither has been tied to Sweeney herself.

What's next: The main open question is whether Sweeney or her representatives will respond. For now, no comment from her has been reported. Attention will also stay on how the film performs after the premiere and whether Baron Cohen or the studio addresses the backlash.
