Sydney Sweeney's Most Overlooked Role Is On HBO

SlashFilm highlighted Sydney Sweeney's Sharp Objects role as one of her best, and as one of her most overlooked performances.

By Sweeney Report · November 14, 2026

SlashFilm published a piece on November 10, 2026 arguing that Sydney Sweeney's work in the HBO miniseries Sharp Objects is among her best and most overlooked performances. The 2018 series was based on Gillian Flynn's novel. Sweeney played Alice, a teenage psychiatric patient. The show earned eight Emmy nominations and a 92 percent score on Rotten Tomatoes, according to SlashFilm.

The role

SlashFilm described Sharp Objects as an eight-episode psychological thriller adapted by Marti Noxon from Flynn's 2006 book. Amy Adams starred as Camille Preaker, a crime reporter who returned to her Missouri hometown of Wind Gap to cover the murders of two young girls.

Sweeney's Alice appeared in flashbacks to Camille's time in a psychiatric facility. According to SlashFilm, Alice coped with her struggles by retreating into her iPod, and the two characters formed a bond during their stay.

How the part grew

SlashFilm cited an earlier Harper's Bazaar interview in which Sweeney said director Jean-Marc Vallée kept bringing her back for more scenes. She said he added her to the story here and there, so the two built the character together.

Harper's Bazaar also reported, per SlashFilm, that Sweeney prepared by visiting hospitals and spending time with patients who had self-harmed.

The schedule was demanding. SlashFilm said Sweeney told Nylon that she shot her Sharp Objects scenes on weekends. She then took a red-eye flight to Portland to film Netflix's Everything Sucks!.

Why it matters now

SlashFilm noted that most viewers know Sweeney for Euphoria, Anyone But You and The Housemaid. It pointed out that some of her other work has also slipped past audiences. It cited her 2025 film Americana, which it said was ignored at the box office, and the drama Clementine, which it said arrived quietly during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns.

The outlet said Sharp Objects was far less of a cultural phenomenon than Euphoria. It added that the series still drew widespread praise on release and brought Sweeney significant attention for the part.

What's next

No new project was announced in connection with the SlashFilm piece. It does show renewed interest in Sweeney's earlier work, and Sharp Objects remains an HBO series that viewers can revisit. Which Sweeney role do you think deserves more attention?
