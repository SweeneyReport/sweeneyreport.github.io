Sydney Sweeney's Novig Ad: Lost 200K Followers, Then Rebounded

Sydney Sweeney reportedly lost over 200,000 Instagram followers after her Novig ad, then won back about 100,000, according to Newsweek.

By Sweeney Report · November 8, 2026

Sydney Sweeney lost more than 200,000 Instagram followers within two days of appearing in an ad for Novig, a sports prediction market valued at about $500 million. According to Newsweek, which cited Social Blade data, she then regained roughly 100,000 of them. The swing has fueled a debate over whether the backlash hurt her or simply kept her name in the headlines.

The Novig ad and the follower drop

Novig is a sports prediction platform, and the campaign put Sweeney at the center of a new commercial partnership. Soon after the ad appeared, her Instagram follower count fell sharply, by more than 200,000 over about 48 hours. Follower totals on large accounts often move for many reasons, so the figures show a change in numbers but do not explain why each person left.

Athletes react

The ad also drew responses from the sports world. Olympic swimmer Ariarne Titmus said she was infuriated by it. Sprinter Amy Hunt addressed the topic in comments on the BBC. Both reactions were personal opinions from athletes, and they added to the attention around the campaign. Sweeney has not been shown to have changed course because of them.

A partial rebound

Newsweek reported, citing Social Blade, that Sweeney gained back about 100,000 followers after the initial loss. That left her still below her earlier total, but it showed the decline had not continued. Social Blade is a third-party tracking service, and its numbers are estimates rather than official figures from Instagram.

Those numbers leave open what the episode means for her. One reading is that the ad cost her part of her audience. Another is that the controversy drew new attention and brought some followers back. The data alone does not settle it, and neither Sweeney nor Novig has been reported to have measured the campaign's wider effect.

This is not the first time an ad has put Sweeney at the center of public discussion. Her endorsement deals have regularly drawn strong reactions, which has made the commercial side of her career a recurring news topic alongside her film and television work.

What's next

What's next: Watch whether her follower count keeps recovering and whether Novig continues the campaign. Any further comment from Sweeney, her team or the athletes involved would add context. For now the picture is mixed: a steep loss, a partial rebound and an open question about whether controversial ads help or hurt a star.
