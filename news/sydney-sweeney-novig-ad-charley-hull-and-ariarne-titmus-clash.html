Sydney Sweeney's Novig Ad Splits Athletes: Hull vs Titmus

Golfer Charley Hull defended Sydney Sweeney's Novig betting ad while swimmer Ariarne Titmus stood by her earlier criticism, dividing athletes publicly.

By Sweeney Report · October 24, 2026

Athletes have publicly split over a sports betting advertisement starring Sydney Sweeney. Swimmer Ariarne Titmus criticized the promotion for Novig and, according to the Toowoomba Chronicle, said she does not regret her comments. Golfer Charley Hull took the opposite view, reportedly telling OutKick she did not understand the backlash.

The background

The ad promotes Novig, a sports betting company. Sweeney reportedly holds a stake in the business, which has added to the discussion around the campaign. Several outlets, including Business Upturn and Cinemablend, described the spot as controversial and covered how it landed with the sporting world.

Titmus stands by her criticism

Titmus reportedly described the ad as a kick in the face to women who had devoted their lives to sport. The Toowoomba Chronicle reported that she later doubled down, saying she did not regret what she said. Her remarks came as one of the more prominent responses from an elite athlete.

Hull pushes back

Hull offered a different perspective. Business Upturn reported that she defended Sweeney and called out athletes who criticized her. BollywoodShaadis quoted her as saying the promotion was literally part of Sweeney's job. Hull also reportedly argued, in her OutKick comments, that around eighty percent of the company's audience is male anyway, which she appeared to suggest made the criticism misplaced. Cinemablend noted that Hull is also a model, and framed her as one professional athlete willing to speak up for the actor.

Why it matters

The exchange shows how Sweeney's endorsement and business activity can spark wider debate, this time inside the sports community. Opinions in the dispute belong to the individuals who voiced them, and neither Hull nor Titmus speaks for athletes as a group.

What's next: Neither Sweeney nor Novig had, in the coverage reviewed, responded publicly to the athletes' comments. It remains to be seen whether the debate continues or whether more athletes weigh in on the campaign.
