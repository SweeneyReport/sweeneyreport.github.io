Sydney Sweeney as the Next Bond Girl? A 007 Star Weighs In

Trina Parks, the first Black Bond girl, told The Telegraph that Sydney Sweeney would be a great fit for the next 007 film, though no casting is official.

By Sweeney Report · November 3, 2026

Trina Parks, a veteran of the Bond franchise, has publicly backed Sydney Sweeney to appear in the next 007 film. Parks played the assassin Thumper opposite Sean Connery in Diamonds Are Forever (1971). She told The Telegraph that Sweeney "would do a wonderful job." No casting for the next film has been officially announced.

What Parks said

According to Samaa TV and TAG24, Parks pointed to the range of roles Sweeney has taken on. She also described the actress as having a classic Hollywood style and poise, qualities Parks said suit Sweeney's projects and her brand.

Parks was the first Black actress to play a Bond girl. TAG24 reported that she is 78. The outlet described Sweeney as 29.

Parks also spoke about how the franchise should treat its female characters. Samaa TV reported that she believes modern audiences want a woman who is independent and has her own voice, not one who is simply won over by Bond. She suggested the next film should give 007 a love interest whose affection he has to earn.

The state of the 007 casting

The comments came as speculation about the next Bond continued. Daniel Craig left the role after No Time to Die (2021). Samaa TV reported that Jack Lowden, Callum Turner and Josh O'Connor have been linked to the part. TAG24 reportedly named Lowden and Turner as the front-runners after a recent screen test. Neither outlet reported an official confirmation.

Samaa TV also noted that Ben Whishaw, who played Q, has said the franchise needs to be reinvented. The report said producers have not confirmed a release date, the new 007 or his love interest.

Sweeney's earlier Bond comments

TAG24 recalled that Sweeney was asked about Bond in an October 2025 interview with Variety. Asked whether she would want to be in the next film, she reportedly answered, "Depends on the script. I think I'd have more fun as James Bond."

Her answer suggests she sees herself in a different role from the one Parks had in mind. There has been no report that she has been approached for any part in the franchise.

What's next

The casting of 007 remains unconfirmed, and no leading lady has been named. Any official announcement would come from the producers. Until then, Parks's endorsement is one veteran's opinion, not a casting report.
