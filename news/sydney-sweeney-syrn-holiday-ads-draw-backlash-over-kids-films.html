Sydney Sweeney's SYRN Holiday Ads Spark Backlash Over Kids' Movies

Critics reportedly accused Sydney Sweeney's SYRN brand of sexualizing children's film characters in a Christmas campaign, sparking a wider debate.

By Sweeney Report · October 25, 2026

Sydney Sweeney's SYRN holiday campaign drew criticism after the lingerie brand recreated characters from well-known children's films. The Sun reported that critics accused the brand of sexualizing a beloved childhood Christmas character. Other outlets, including BGNES, the Toowoomba Chronicle and TV Shows Ace, reportedly covered similar complaints, and the debate spread quickly online.

The campaign

According to the reports, the SYRN Christmas campaign riffed on familiar holiday stories, reportedly drawing on the Grinch, Cindy Lou Who, Home Alone and Dr. Seuss. The Toowoomba Chronicle described the concept as a recreation of kids' movies in a lingerie advertisement. TV Shows Ace focused on the Cindy Lou Who-themed shoot, which it described as provocative.

The brand's marketing has regularly leaned on pop-culture references and seasonal themes, and a holiday release built around nostalgic characters fits that approach. The sources reviewed for this article did not include a statement from SYRN or from Sweeney explaining the creative choices.

The reaction

The Sun reported that critics said the campaign sexualized a character associated with childhood. BGNES ran a headline saying Sweeney was accused of sexualizing child characters from popular films. The Toowoomba Chronicle likewise described a backlash erupting over the ads.

The specific critics quoted in those pieces were not independently verified here, and the scale of the reaction is based on the outlets' own descriptions. Some of the coverage comes from tabloid-style outlets, so the strength of the backlash may be characterized differently depending on the source.

The wider debate

The episode raises a familiar question in advertising: where the line sits when adult-oriented brands borrow from family entertainment. Critics argue that using characters from children's stories blurs that line. Supporters of such campaigns, as is typical in these debates, often describe them as playful holiday marketing and parody. Neither side's position was confirmed by SYRN in the material available.

What's next: It remains unclear whether SYRN or Sweeney will respond publicly, or whether the brand will adjust the campaign. Any official comment, changes to the ads or reaction from rights holders of the referenced titles would be significant developments to watch.
