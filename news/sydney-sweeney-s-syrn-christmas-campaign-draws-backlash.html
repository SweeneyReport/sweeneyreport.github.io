Sydney Sweeney's Syrn Christmas Campaign Sparks Backlash

Sydney Sweeney's Syrn holiday campaign drew criticism over its Cindy Lou Who look, while some supporters called the reaction exaggerated.

By Sweeney Report · November 7, 2026

Sydney Sweeney's new holiday campaign for her Syrn brand drew a wave of online criticism this week, with its Cindy Lou Who concept from The Grinch becoming the most debated part of the rollout. TheRichest reported on November 6 that critics accused her of sexualizing a child character, while some supporters said the backlash was blown out of proportion.

What the campaign was

According to TheRichest, the Christmas promotion for Syrn drew on well-known holiday favorites and pop-culture characters. The outlet said one of the first concepts was a take on Jack Skellington from The Nightmare Before Christmas. The Sun also covered the campaign, publishing its own roundup of the holiday concepts and framing the release as a seasonal talking point.

Syrn is Sweeney's own brand, and the holiday push was reportedly built around playful riffs on familiar characters rather than a traditional product announcement.

Why the Cindy Lou Who concept drew fire

TheRichest reported that the Cindy Lou Who concept attracted the most public criticism. In its account, critics argued that the character, who is a child in the original story, was an inappropriate choice for an adult fashion campaign. The outlet said some supporters pushed back, arguing that the reaction was exaggerated and that the campaign was meant as holiday fun.

Neither Sweeney nor Syrn had publicly responded to the criticism in the material reviewed for this article. TheRichest did not report any change to the campaign.

A recent history of backlash

TheRichest also tied the reaction to an earlier controversy. The outlet recalled her advertisements for the sports-prediction platform Novig, which it said drew sharp criticism, including from women athletes. According to TheRichest, she lost more than 200,000 Instagram followers after those ads. That figure comes from the outlet's report, and it was not independently verified here.

The comparison suggests that Sweeney's marketing choices, whether for her own brand or for outside partners, have become a regular subject of public debate.

What's next

It remained unclear whether the Syrn campaign would change in response to the criticism or whether the brand would address the Cindy Lou Who reaction directly. The debate was still unfolding online as of November 7, and further statements from Sweeney or Syrn would show whether the backlash affects the rest of the holiday rollout.
