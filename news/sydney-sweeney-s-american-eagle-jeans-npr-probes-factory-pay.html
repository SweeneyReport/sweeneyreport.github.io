Sydney Sweeney's American Eagle Jeans: Who Makes Them? NPR Reports

NPR traveled to Karachi to ask who makes the denim from Sydney Sweeney's American Eagle campaign, and spoke with a worker paid about $144 a month.

By Sweeney Report · November 17, 2026

NPR reported on November 15, 2026 that a garment worker in Karachi, Pakistan, says she has worked on American Eagle production lines and is paid about $144 a month. The woman, a single mother, asked to be identified only as S. because she feared being fired. NPR's story asked a question that the debate over Sydney Sweeney's American Eagle campaign largely skipped: who actually makes the jeans?

What the worker told NPR

According to NPR correspondent Diaa Hadid, S. recognized the denim styles shown in the campaign because she had worked on them and on similar models. "I know how all of them are made, because I have worked on them," she told NPR.

NPR reported that S. works at a factory run by a company called Artistic Garment Industries. She said her pay of roughly $144 a month supports her son and her mother. She also described fear of speaking out. "If you raise your voice, they'll push you out," she told NPR, adding, "We are helpless."

NPR's report also looked at working conditions in garment factories in Pakistan and Bangladesh, noting that most clothing factories operate in Asia and that most American shoppers never see the people who make their clothes.

The campaign and the earlier debate

The American Eagle advertisement launched in July 2025 with the tagline "Sydney Sweeney has great jeans." NPR reported that it set off a public argument over whether the wording was a veiled reference to eugenics, a reading some critics put forward. In a September 2025 statement, American Eagle said the campaign "is and always was about the jeans," according to NPR.

Responses

NPR reported that Sweeney did not respond to its request for comment. The excerpt of the story available to us did not include a response from Artistic Garment Industries, and we could not independently verify S.'s account of her pay or conditions.

What's next

It was not clear whether American Eagle or Artistic Garment Industries would respond to the worker's claims, or whether the report would lead to any review of the brand's supply chain. Sweeney Report will update this story if either company or Sweeney comments.
