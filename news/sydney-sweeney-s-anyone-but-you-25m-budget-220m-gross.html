Sydney Sweeney's Anyone But You: $25M Budget, $220M Gross

Anyone But You, the Sydney Sweeney and Glen Powell rom-com, turned a $25 million budget into roughly $220 million worldwide.

By Sweeney Report · October 28, 2026

Anyone But You, the romantic comedy starring Sydney Sweeney and Glen Powell, turned a reported production budget of $25 million into a worldwide gross of roughly $220 million. Directed by Will Gluck, the film reached theaters in December 2023. It earned close to nine times its cost, according to figures published by The Numbers. Sweeney also worked on the film as an executive producer.

From a modest budget to a sleeper hit

Wikipedia's entry on the film says it premiered in New York on December 11, 2023. Sony Pictures Releasing opened it in the United States and Canada on December 22. The same entry says it opened in fourth place with about $6.3 million. Weekend totals then held steady, and the third weekend rose about 9% to roughly $9.5 million. Such a pattern is unusual for a film's early run.

The Numbers lists a $25 million production budget. It puts the domestic total at about $88.3 million and the international total at around $130 million. Worldwide, that comes to about $219 million, or 8.8 times the budget. Totals differ slightly between pages and update dates. Wikipedia rounds the figure to about $220 million and says Deadline estimated a net profit of $103 million.

The film kept earning well into 2024. Wikipedia says an extended version returned to theaters on February 9, 2024, ahead of Valentine's Day. Digital release followed on February 20, and Blu-ray on March 12. TheWrap reported that it would also reach Netflix in April 2024.

Critics and audiences

Reviews were mixed. Wikipedia reports a 53% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes, based on 118 reviews, and a Metacritic score of 52. Audiences polled by CinemaScore gave it a B+. The box office run suggests that viewers responded more warmly than many critics did.

The film is loosely based on Shakespeare's Much Ado About Nothing. Wikipedia credits Gluck, Joe Roth and Jeff Kirschenbaum as producers. Other outlets report that Sweeney was an executive producer.

The sequel talk

Sweeney has repeatedly been asked about a follow-up. In a 2025 interview with Empire, she reportedly said it was "not a no, and it's not a yes." On The Tonight Show, she reportedly put the chances at "maybe like a high 9." The Hollywood Reporter reported that Powell said the two were still talking about the idea. As far as the sources reviewed show, no sequel has been officially announced.

What's next: No sequel is confirmed. Sweeney and Powell have only said publicly that they are still talking about working together again. Any announcement would most likely come from Sony or from the actors themselves.
