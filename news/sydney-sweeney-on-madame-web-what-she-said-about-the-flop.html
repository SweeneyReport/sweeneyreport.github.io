Sydney Sweeney on Madame Web: What She Really Said

Sydney Sweeney addressed the backlash to Madame Web, calling it a strategic business decision and saying she would return for the right story.

By Sweeney Report · November 16, 2026

Sydney Sweeney spoke publicly in 2024 about Madame Web, the critically panned Sony film that marked her entry into the Spider-Man universe. She told the Los Angeles Times she was "just hired as an actress" and "along for the ride." In a separate GQ interview, she called the project a strategic business decision and said she would be open to coming back as Spider-Woman.

The film and her role

Madame Web premiered in Los Angeles on February 12, 2024, and opened in the United States on February 14. It was directed by S. J. Clarkson, in her feature directing debut, and distributed by Sony Pictures Releasing. Dakota Johnson played Cassandra Webb. Sweeney played Julia Cornwall, a teenager who is one of three young women destined to become Spider-Women. Isabela Merced and Celeste O'Connor played the other two.

A poor reception

Critics were harsh. According to Wikipedia's summary of the reviews, the film holds a 10 percent score on Rotten Tomatoes and 26 out of 100 on Metacritic. The same page says it grossed roughly $100.5 million worldwide against a reported budget in the low $100 million range, which made it a box-office disappointment. It later won three Golden Raspberry Awards, including Worst Picture. Sweeney was not among the nominees named on that page.

What Sweeney said

While promoting her horror film Immaculate, Sweeney was asked about the backlash. The Los Angeles Times quoted her saying: "I was just hired as an actress in it, so I was just along for the ride for whatever was going to happen." Variety and other outlets picked up the comment in March 2024.

In a GQ interview, she took a more career-minded view. She described the film as a building block and said that without it she would not have a relationship with the decision-makers at Sony. She said such choices were strategic business decisions, and she tied the experience to selling Anyone But You and landing Barbarella, according to coverage of the interview.

Asked about playing the character again, Sweeney reportedly told GQ: "I think that if the story is right and you have the right team, I would love to." Heroic Hollywood said this applied to a possible sequel or a Spider-Woman project.

What's next

No sequel or Spider-Woman project featuring Sweeney has been confirmed in the sources reviewed. Her comments suggest that any return would depend on the creative team and the story. Would you want to see her back as Spider-Woman?
