Sydney Sweeney Bond Girl Praise, But Odds Say Third Place

A 007 star reportedly said Sydney Sweeney would make a wonderful Bond girl, but Kalshi odds put her third behind Margaret Qualley and Anya Taylor-Joy.

By Sweeney Report · October 31, 2026

Sydney Sweeney received public praise as a possible Bond girl, but the betting odds were less generous. The Telegraph reported that a 007 star said she would make a wonderful Bond girl. News24 reported that, according to the prediction market Kalshi, she was only third in the race, with a 19 percent chance.

The Kalshi numbers

News24 reported that her former Once Upon a Time in Hollywood co-star Margaret Qualley led the field at 39 percent. Anya Taylor-Joy followed at 26 percent. Sweeney came next at 19 percent, and Florence Pugh was fourth at 13 percent.

News24 noted that Sweeney and Qualley both had fairly small parts in the Quentin Tarantino film and have since become major names.

Why Qualley's name surged

According to News24, speculation about Qualley started after she led a choreographed dance during the James Bond tribute at the 97th Academy Awards. The outlet described her lead in the odds as comfortable.

News24 also cited the US Sun, which reportedly said Pugh was expected to play Moneypenny in a Bond-related show possibly titled The Moneypenny Diaries. That detail was not confirmed in the material reviewed.

The wider Bond conversation

The casting talk was not limited to the women. News24 reported that Jack Lowden was seen as a leading candidate to play Bond, helped by his role as River Cartwright in Slow Horses. It added that Nina Gold, who cast that series, was set to handle casting for the franchise.

Saoirse Ronan, according to News24, said she was “dead serious” about wanting to play a Bond villain rather than a Bond girl.

The outlet also reported on a debate over whether the term “Bond girl” should still be used, noting that actresses including Lea Seydoux and Monica Bellucci had voiced reservations about it in the past.

Neither Sweeney nor the Bond producers were quoted in the sources on the odds. Kalshi percentages reflect market betting, not any casting decision.

What’s next: No actress had been officially announced for a Bond film at the time of reporting, so the odds could shift as casting news emerges. Sweeney’s name is now part of that conversation, and the Telegraph’s report suggests her industry support is not in doubt, even if the market currently favors others.
